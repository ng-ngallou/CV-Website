Work Experience

  • Dec 2023 - today

    Computing Engineer

    CERN, Geneva, Switzerland

    Contributed to a variety of projects, including improved accelerator performance, simulation code development (BLonD), internal tools migration (Nicejapc). The job requires well-rounded knowledge of the entire RF system, especially the Low Level RF (LLRF) control systems. Participated in commissioning in the underground facilities and on call duty for the LLRF of the SPS and LHC. I was part of the team that significantly improved and stabilized the LHC blow-up process, and the main author on the paper, which I presented in IPAC'25.

    Tasks/Projects
    • Active participation in the SPS and LHC particle accelerators Low Power-Level RF (LLRF) re-commissioning after the annual Year-End Technical Stop in the underground facilities (Faraday cage) and remotely (control room).
    • Maintenance and further development of the LLRF Python packages, containing the commissioning scripts for SPS and LHC, as well as the tool packages LLRF and NiceJapc. The commissioning scripts include system response measurements, as well as calibrations. They interact with the hardware through CERN’s python device interface.
    • Development of the repository for the SPS Crab Cavities, which contains open and closed loop measurement of the system, in line with the other LLRF packages.
    • Migration of NiceJapc backend from PyJapc to PyDA (CERN’s python device interfaces).
    • Participation in the on-call duty (shadowing experts) for the LLRF of LINAC3, LINAC4, SPS, LHC particle accelerators. Experience in interventions.
    • Downloading and processing data from NXCALS (CERN’s database) for model training and simulations.
    • Main researcher on the LHC blow-up issue, which was stabilized, improving LHC operation significantly. The study included particle beam dynamics simulations using BLonD code, data analysis and testing of the results using dedicated beams on the accelerator (LHC). Authored paper: "Bunch length regulation in the LHC during controlled emittance blow-up", and presented poster in IPAC’25 (Taiwan).
    • Contribution to Beam Longitudinal Dynamics (BLonD) simulation code for more accurate implementation of phase noise injection in the LHC. Co-authored paper: "Recent Developments of Blond for New Large Scale Accelerator Facilities", S. Lauber et al., which was presented in IPAC'26 (France).
    • Developed and implemented an AutoEncoder for LINAC4 RF voltage breakdown diagnostics. It labels live pulses as "good" or "bad" and provides a score. It has been generalized to work on all 20 LN4 RF devices (some of which function at different set-points). At the moment it is being tested live on the accelerator.
